# Content Strategy

## 1. Overview

The website will bring together Joe Waldron's academic achievements, software development work, automotive and mechanical projects, experience, and professional information.

The content should demonstrate both the breadth of Joe's interests and the depth of his engineering ability. It should not simply list achievements. Wherever possible, important claims should be supported by evidence such as project documentation, images, testing results, source code, or other relevant material.

The content structure should also support the long-term development of the website. New projects, achievements, experiences and other information should be addable through the CMS without requiring changes to the website's source code.

---

## 2. Core Website Content

The initial website will contain the following main sections:

- Home

- Projects

- About Me

- Education

- Experience

- Contact

These sections represent the core information that an employer should be able to access when evaluating Joe.

The navigation should remain deliberately simple. Individual project pages will be accessible from the Projects section but will not appear as separate items in the main navigation.

---

## 3. Homepage Content

The homepage is the most important page for creating an immediate impression.

It should communicate the following information prominently:

- Joe Waldron's name.

- His connection to software engineering and the automotive industry.

- His strongest current achievement or selling point.

- His currently featured project.

- A concise introduction.

- Direct routes to contact and professional profiles.

The featured project should be managed through the CMS rather than being permanently hardcoded into the homepage.

The homepage should provide enough information to create curiosity while directing interested visitors towards deeper content.

The content presented on the homepage should therefore be concise compared with the detailed project and experience pages.

---

## 4. About Me Content

The About Me section should provide more personal context than the other sections of the website.

It should communicate:

- Joe's interests.

- His motivation for engineering.

- His interest in software and automotive technology.

- His approach to learning and problem solving.

- Relevant personal characteristics.

- His broader career direction.

This section should make the website feel like an introduction to a person rather than simply an online record of achievements.

It should complement the more evidence-based project and education sections rather than repeating their content.

---

## 5. Education Content

The Education section will contain Joe's academic background and significant academic achievements.

Each education milestone should be capable of containing:

- Institution.

- Qualification or programme.

- Subjects or relevant coursework.

- Dates.

- Grades or results where appropriate.

- Relevant projects or achievements.

- A description of the significance of the milestone.

Academic achievements should be presented alongside relevant evidence where possible.

The Extended Project Qualification should be included as a significant academic achievement. Its research into the relationship between public perception of autonomous vehicles and the safety features present in Level 3 and above autonomous vehicles is particularly relevant to Joe's wider interest in automotive technology.

The education content should be expandable over time as further qualifications, results and achievements are obtained.

---

## 6. Project Content

Projects are one of the most important content types on the website.

They should provide evidence of Joe's ability to apply technical knowledge to practical problems.

The initial portfolio is expected to include approximately:

### Software Projects

- A Level Computer Science coursework — engine simulator.

- Portfolio website.

- Audio tachometer.

- AI development environment.

- Driving lesson simulator or object model reconstructor.

### Automotive and Mechanical Projects

- Yamasaki F50 rebuild.

- Yamaha R125 rebuild.

- RS125 rebuild.

- 90cc engine rebuild.

The project structure should support future projects without requiring a new page template to be developed for every project.

---

## 7. Project Categories

Projects should be organised into broad categories so that visitors can understand the different areas of work.

The initial categories are expected to include:

- Software.

- Automotive.

- Research.

The exact category system should remain flexible enough to accommodate future work.

Projects should also support tags so that more specific topics can be represented without creating excessive categories.

For example, a software project could use tags such as:

- JavaScript.

- Python.

- AI.

- Web development.

- Simulation.

An automotive project could use tags such as:

- Motorcycle.

- Engine.

- Restoration.

- Electronics.

- Mechanical engineering.

Categories provide the main structure while tags provide more detailed filtering.

---

## 8. Project Evidence

Project content should prioritise evidence over unsupported claims.

Depending on the project, this may include:

- Images.

- Videos.

- Audio.

- Testing results.

- Diagrams.

- Documentation.

- Source code.

- GitHub repositories.

- External links.

- Technical explanations.

- Results and measurements.

Projects should be capable of presenting different types of evidence because software, mechanical and research projects do not necessarily produce the same outputs.

Media should therefore be treated as part of the project content rather than as an afterthought.

---

## 9. Project Documentation

Project pages should support a flexible structure rather than forcing every project into the same format.

A substantial software project may contain detailed information about:

- The problem.

- Research.

- Planning.

- Design.

- Implementation.

- Testing.

- Results.

- Challenges.

- Future improvements.

A smaller project or individual feature may only require a short description, an image and a link to the source code.

The content system should therefore allow sections to be included when relevant and omitted when they would add unnecessary information.

Mechanical projects may also require a different style of documentation. These projects often involve multiple problems being discovered and resolved during the work, with solutions adapting to available tools, components and defects.

The content strategy should accommodate this difference rather than forcing mechanical projects to follow a software development methodology that does not accurately represent the work.

---

## 10. Experience Content

The Experience section should record relevant professional and practical experience.

Experience may include different types of activities, including:

- Employment.

- Work experience.

- Talks.

- Workshops.

- Other relevant engineering or technical activities.

Different experience types may require different information, so the CMS should eventually support multiple experience formats while maintaining a consistent presentation on the public website.

Experience should focus on what was learned, what responsibilities were held and why the experience is relevant rather than simply providing a chronological list of positions.

---

## 11. Skills Content

A skills section should eventually provide an overview of relevant technical and engineering abilities.

Skills may include:

- Programming languages.

- Frameworks and technologies.

- Software development practices.

- Engineering skills.

- Automotive and mechanical skills.

- Other relevant technical abilities.

Skills should be managed through the CMS so that they can be updated as Joe develops.

The presentation should avoid implying a level of expertise that is not supported by the projects and experience presented elsewhere on the site.

---

## 12. Contact Content

The Contact section should provide a straightforward way for visitors to make contact.

The initial implementation should include:

- A working contact form.

- Relevant contact details.

- LinkedIn.

- GitHub.

- Other appropriate professional contact methods.

The contact experience should require as few interactions as reasonably possible.

The website should not force visitors to create an account or navigate through unnecessary pages to make contact.

A more advanced centralised contact management system may be introduced later through the CMS.

---

## 13. CV and Professional Profiles

The website should make Joe's CV easily accessible.

Links to professional profiles should also be available, particularly:

- LinkedIn.

- GitHub.

These should be accessible from the homepage and other relevant locations without overwhelming the main content.

The CV and external profiles provide additional evidence and should complement rather than replace the content hosted on the portfolio.

---

## 14. Temporary Website Announcements

The website should support temporary banners or announcements.

These should be used for information that is currently relevant but should not become permanent website content.

Potential uses include:

- Highlighting a new project.

- Announcing a major project milestone.

- Drawing attention to an active development project.

- Highlighting current applications or opportunities.

- Announcing significant updates to the website.

Announcements should be manageable through the CMS and should be capable of being removed or replaced without requiring code changes.

The purpose of these banners is to make the website feel current rather than to create permanent promotional content.

---

## 15. Content Freshness

The website should clearly distinguish between current and historical information where appropriate.

Projects should contain a publication date or last updated date, allowing visitors to understand how recently content was changed.

Recently updated projects may be visually identified where appropriate.

The homepage should always be capable of highlighting the most relevant current work rather than being permanently tied to the oldest or first projects added to the portfolio.

---

## 16. Content Management Principles

Content should be structured so that routine updates can be completed through the CMS.

The CMS should eventually allow Joe to:

- Add and edit projects.

- Add education achievements.

- Add experience.

- Update skills.

- Change featured projects.

- Manage temporary announcements.

- Update profile images.

- Update contact details.

- Manage social media connections.

- Add future content types.

Content should be separated from the website's presentation wherever practical.

The frontend should therefore provide reusable layouts that can display structured content without requiring a new page to be manually developed for each item.

---

## 17. Content Quality Principles

The website content should follow several principles:

### Evidence over claims

Technical ability should be demonstrated through work and evidence wherever possible.

### Concise at the top level

Important information should be understandable without reading an entire page.

### Depth where valuable

Detailed technical information should be available when it adds value.

### Flexible structure

Different types of projects should not be forced into identical formats.

### Current information

The homepage and temporary announcements should be capable of reflecting current projects and career activity.

### Maintainable content

Routine content changes should be possible through the CMS without requiring source-code changes.

### Professional but personal

Content should demonstrate technical ability while retaining the friendly and approachable character established in the overall vision.

---

## 18. Future Content

The content architecture should leave room for additional content types without requiring a fundamental redesign.

Potential future content includes:

- Development logs.

- Blog posts.

- Project demonstrations.

- Additional academic achievements.

- Expanded professional experience.

- Accessibility information.

- Social media updates.

- Recruiter-focused information.

- AI-assisted information discovery.

These are not part of the initial content requirements unless explicitly prioritised later.

The initial content strategy should focus on building a strong foundation that can support these additions without allowing future possibilities to create unnecessary scope for Version 1.
