# Audience

## 1. Overview

The website has been designed primarily for employers who may be considering Joe Waldron for software engineering, degree apprenticeship, or other technical opportunities.

The content and user experience should therefore prioritise professional visitors while remaining accessible and interesting to people with different levels of technical knowledge.

There are three main audience groups:

1. **Primary audience — software engineering recruiters and hiring managers**

2. **Secondary audience — software engineers and technical professionals**

3. **Secondary audience — universities and admissions staff**

The website should not be designed around a single level of technical knowledge. Instead, the information architecture should allow visitors to understand the important points quickly while providing opportunities to investigate technical details further.

---

## 2. Primary Audience — Recruiters and Hiring Managers

The primary audience consists of software engineering recruiters and hiring managers, particularly those recruiting for automotive, motorsport and technology-related roles.

These visitors are likely to have limited time when reviewing candidates and may initially be comparing a large number of applications.

The website should therefore make it possible for a recruiter to quickly determine:

- Who Joe is.

- What he is studying.

- His strongest academic achievements.

- His most impressive projects.

- His technical skills.

- His relevant experience.

- His interest in the automotive industry.

- How to contact him.

- Where to find his CV, LinkedIn and GitHub.

### Information needs

Recruiters should be able to understand the significance of a project without needing to read the entire project page.

The highest-level project information should therefore communicate:

- What the project is.

- What problem it addresses.

- Why it is interesting.

- What Joe's role was.

- What technologies or engineering methods were involved.

- What was achieved.

- Where further evidence can be found.

The first layer of information should be visually engaging enough to create curiosity. A recruiter who initially intends to review one project should be encouraged to explore further if the project demonstrates something genuinely interesting.

### Desired experience

The recruiter journey should be fast and clear.

The website should avoid forcing recruiters to navigate through large amounts of information before reaching the relevant content or contact methods.

Important actions such as viewing the CV, opening LinkedIn, visiting GitHub and contacting Joe should remain easily accessible.

---

## 3. Secondary Audience — Software Engineers and Technical Professionals

The second major audience consists of software engineers, technical professionals and other people with a strong understanding of software or engineering concepts.

These visitors are more likely to be interested in how projects were actually developed rather than simply what the final result was.

The website should therefore provide substantially more depth beneath the initial information layer.

Technical visitors should be able to investigate:

- Problem definitions.

- Research.

- Planning.

- Architecture.

- Design decisions.

- Implementation.

- Testing.

- Results.

- Challenges.

- Technical documentation.

- Source code.

- Development logs.

- Future improvements.

Project pages should provide enough evidence for a technically experienced visitor to understand how Joe approaches engineering problems and how effectively the proposed solution was implemented.

### Desired experience

A technical visitor should be able to start with the same featured project as a recruiter but follow a deeper path through the available information.

A typical journey may be:

```text
Homepage
    ↓
Featured Project
    ↓
Gallery / Evidence
    ↓
Project Overview
    ↓
Technical Details
    ↓
Documentation / Development Log
    ↓
GitHub
```

This means the website should support both quick scanning and deep exploration without creating two completely separate versions of the same content.

---

## 4. Secondary Audience — Universities and Admissions Staff

Universities and admissions staff form another secondary audience.

These visitors may be interested in:

- Academic performance.

- Subjects studied.

- Educational history.

- Academic projects.

- Extended Project Qualification.

- Technical interests.

- Evidence of independent learning.

- Evidence of problem solving.

- Personal development.

The education section should therefore provide a clear overview of Joe's academic background while linking relevant achievements and projects where appropriate.

The website should demonstrate that academic achievements are supported by practical application rather than presenting grades as isolated statistics.

---

## 5. Different Levels of Technical Knowledge

The website should not assume that every visitor has the same technical knowledge.

The primary information layer should use clear language and avoid unnecessary technical terminology where it does not add value.

Technical explanations can then become progressively more detailed.

A project page should therefore be capable of following a structure similar to:

```text
Level 1 — Quick understanding
What is this project and why is it interesting?

        ↓

Level 2 — Engineering overview
What problem was solved and what approach was taken?

        ↓

Level 3 — Technical explanation
How was the solution designed and implemented?

        ↓

Level 4 — Evidence
Testing, results, documentation, source code and development history
```

This structure allows a recruiter to stop after the first one or two levels while allowing an engineer to continue into the technical details.

---

## 6. Technical Information Presentation

Technical information should be presented in a way that demonstrates competence without unnecessarily excluding non-technical visitors.

Where appropriate, the website may use:

- Expandable technical explanations.

- Tooltips or inserts for terminology.

- Clearly separated technical sections.

- Short summaries before detailed explanations.

- Diagrams.

- Images and other visual evidence.

- Links to documentation or source code.

These features should only be used where they improve comprehension. They should not become a distraction or make the interface unnecessarily complicated.

The goal is not to hide technical information from recruiters, but to make its significance understandable without requiring specialist knowledge.

---

## 7. Audience Information Hierarchy

The website should use a layered information hierarchy.

### First layer — Immediate impression

This should communicate:

- Joe Waldron.

- His strongest achievement.

- His strongest current project.

- His connection to software engineering and the automotive industry.

- Easy access to contact and professional profiles.

### Second layer — Portfolio overview

This should provide:

- Projects.

- Education.

- Experience.

- Skills.

- About information.

### Third layer — Detailed evidence

This should provide:

- Project methodologies.

- Technical explanations.

- Testing.

- Results.

- Documentation.

- Development logs.

- GitHub repositories.

This hierarchy allows the same website to satisfy the needs of visitors who spend 30 seconds on the site and visitors who spend significantly longer investigating the work.

---

## 8. Shared Audience Goals

Despite their different requirements, all audiences should be able to establish several key facts quickly:

- Joe has strong academic ability.

- Joe can apply computer science knowledge to practical problems.

- Joe has experience developing real projects.

- Joe is interested in automotive engineering and technology.

- Joe can investigate and solve problems independently.

- Joe can communicate technical work effectively.

- Joe is actively developing his skills.

The website should use projects and evidence to demonstrate these points rather than relying solely on statements about capability.

---

## 9. Audience Design Principle

The website should be designed around the principle of:

> **Simple to understand, deep enough to investigate.**

The first layer should be eye-catching and accessible enough to encourage exploration.

The deeper layers should contain enough technical evidence to satisfy an experienced engineer who wants to understand how the work was actually completed.

This approach allows the website to remain approachable to recruiters while still functioning as a serious technical portfolio for engineers and other technically experienced visitors.
