# Vision and Purpose

## 1. Overview

The Joe Waldron Portfolio Website is a dynamic portfolio and professional online presence designed to bring together academic achievements, software projects, mechanical projects, experience, and relevant personal information in one accessible location.

The website is not intended to function as a simple digital CV or collection of achievements. It is intended to demonstrate how Joe approaches engineering problems by allowing visitors to explore completed work and the evidence behind it.

The website itself will also form part of the portfolio. A custom content management system will allow the content presented on the public website to be updated without requiring routine changes to the source code.

The project therefore has two primary purposes:

1. Present Joe's experience, achievements and work to potential employers.

2. Demonstrate Joe's ability to design and build a complete, functional engineering system.

---

## 2. Primary Purpose

The primary purpose of the website is to provide employers with an accessible and engaging way to understand who Joe is, what he has achieved, what he can build, and how he approaches engineering problems.

The primary audience is software engineering recruiters and hiring managers, particularly those working within the automotive and motorsport industries.

The website should allow visitors to:

- Understand who Joe is.

- Identify his strongest achievements.

- Find his most technically impressive projects.

- Understand how he approaches engineering problems.

- See evidence that his projects have been genuinely developed.

- Explore his programming and automotive work.

- Find his education and experience.

- Contact him.

- Connect with him through LinkedIn.

- Explore his code through GitHub.

- Download his CV.

These actions should be possible without requiring visitors to search through large amounts of information first.

---

## 3. Desired Visitor Impression

The website should leave visitors with the impression that Joe is an innovative thinker who can bring valuable ideas and technical ability to an engineering team.

The intended impression is that Joe is:

- Friendly and approachable.

- Innovative.

- Technically capable.

- Adaptable.

- Passionate about engineering and technology.

- Confident without being arrogant.

- Willing to investigate problems rather than simply accept existing solutions.

- Able to apply computer science to real-world engineering problems.

The website should communicate confidence through the quality and evidence of the work presented rather than through exaggerated claims.

The overall impression should be that Joe is a highly capable candidate who can offer useful ideas and contribute meaningfully to an engineering team.

---

## 4. The 30-Second Goal

A visitor who spends approximately 30 seconds on the website should ideally remember four things:

1. **Joe Waldron's name.**

2. **His biggest or most significant achievement.**

3. **His strongest project.**

4. **His ambition to apply his computer science skills within the automotive industry.**

The homepage must therefore communicate these points immediately rather than relying on visitors exploring several pages before understanding who the site represents.

The featured project should be selected through the CMS so that the project representing Joe's strongest current work can change as his portfolio develops.

---

## 5. Desired Visitor Actions

The website should encourage visitors towards several actions, prioritised in the following order:

1. Contact Joe through the website.

2. Connect with him on LinkedIn.

3. Visit his GitHub to explore projects and source code.

4. Download his CV.

These actions should be easily accessible from the homepage and should not require visitors to navigate through several unrelated pages.

The website should also encourage deeper exploration when a visitor becomes interested in a particular project or area of work.

The first layer of information should be visually engaging and technically interesting enough that a visitor who initially intends to view only one project is encouraged to explore further.

---

## 6. Demonstrating Engineering Ability

The website should demonstrate how Joe approaches engineering problems rather than only displaying the final results of his projects.

Project pages should therefore be capable of presenting evidence such as:

- Problems encountered.

- Research undertaken.

- Planning.

- Design decisions.

- Implementation.

- Testing.

- Results.

- Challenges.

- Future improvements.

- Images and other media.

- Technical documentation.

- Source code.

- External resources.

Not every project will require every section. The content model should allow relevant sections to be included while avoiding unnecessary information on smaller projects.

This should allow technically experienced visitors to investigate projects in depth while allowing recruiters and other non-specialist visitors to understand the important points quickly.

Technical explanations should assume that an engineering or software engineering reader is capable of understanding the concepts being discussed. At the same time, the highest-level information should communicate the important ideas clearly enough for a recruiter to understand Joe's technical competence at a glance.

---

## 7. The Website as an Engineering Demonstration

The website itself should form part of Joe's portfolio.

The system will require the design and implementation of:

- A frontend application.

- A backend API.

- A relational database.

- A content management system.

- Authentication.

- Media management.

- Content validation.

- Preview and publishing functionality.

- Automated testing.

- Deployment infrastructure.

- Technical documentation.

These systems should be understandable, maintainable and appropriately complex for the problem being solved.

Established technologies should be used where they provide reliable solutions to difficult or security-sensitive problems. The project should not unnecessarily recreate existing solutions simply for the sake of doing so.

Engineering effort should instead be focused on understanding how the systems work, integrating them effectively, and building the functionality that is specific to the portfolio.

---

## 8. Long-Term Vision

The initial version of the website will focus on establishing a strong and functional portfolio.

Over time, the website is intended to develop into a more complete record of Joe's professional and engineering development.

The long-term vision is for the website to become a central place where someone can understand:

- Who Joe is.

- What he has learned.

- What he has built.

- What he is currently working on.

- How his skills have developed.

- What areas of engineering interest him.

- How he approaches problems.

- Where his career is heading.

Potential future features include development logs, project demonstrations, accessibility features, an improved mobile experience, visitor accounts, automated social media integration, meeting booking, and an AI-assisted system for helping recruiters find relevant information about Joe's experience.

These features are deliberately outside the initial scope unless they can be implemented without putting the core project at risk.

The website should therefore be designed to evolve from an initial portfolio into a long-term record of Joe's engineering development without requiring the system to be rebuilt from scratch.

---

## 9. Success Criteria

The vision of the website will be considered successful if it achieves the following:

- A recruiter can understand who Joe is within a short visit.

- Joe's strongest work is immediately visible.

- Visitors can quickly find evidence of his technical ability.

- Technical visitors can explore projects in significantly greater depth.

- Contact methods are easy to find.

- The website demonstrates the ability to design and build a complete software system.

- New portfolio content can be added without requiring routine changes to the source code.

- The system can grow alongside Joe's education and career.

- The website itself is strong enough to be considered one of Joe's portfolio projects.

Ultimately, the website should do more than tell visitors that Joe is capable of engineering.

**It should provide the evidence to demonstrate it.**
